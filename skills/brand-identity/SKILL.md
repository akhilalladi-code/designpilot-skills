# Brand Identity Skill

## Objective
Create a distinctive, usable identity system—not merely a logo concept. Connect every recommendation to the brand's audience, positioning, personality, and real-world use.

## Required inputs
Ask for missing essentials before making strong assumptions:
- Brand name and pronunciation
- Product, service, industry, and geography
- Primary audience and the audience's needs
- Positioning, differentiators, and competitors
- Desired personality and attributes to avoid
- Existing assets, colors, fonts, and non-negotiable constraints
- Main applications and production requirements

If the user cannot provide all inputs, state assumptions explicitly and continue with a clearly labelled exploratory direction.

## Workflow
1. **Frame the problem.** Summarize the business goal, audience, key message, and constraints in plain language.
2. **Find the strategic territory.** Identify useful positioning themes. Avoid unsupported claims such as "market leader" unless supplied and verifiable.
3. **Explore distinct routes.** Present 2–3 meaningfully different creative territories. For each, explain its idea, visual cues, strengths, and risks.
4. **Define the visual system.** Specify logo behavior, typography roles, color roles, graphic language, imagery, layout principles, and motion where relevant.
5. **Test applications.** Check the direction on realistic touchpoints: small avatar, document, web header, social post, and monochrome use.
6. **Document rules.** Include clear-space guidance, minimum size, misuse examples, contrast guidance, and asset/export needs.
7. **Review consistency.** Confirm that the system feels like one brand across all touchpoints.

## Quality checklist
- Does the concept communicate the intended positioning?
- Is it recognisable without relying on a trendy effect?
- Does it work at small sizes and in one color?
- Are typography and colors practical and accessible?
- Can another designer apply the system consistently?
- Are claims, symbols, and cultural references appropriate for the audience?

## Output format
Return: brief summary; assumptions; 2–3 concept routes; recommended route with rationale; visual-system specification; sample applications; production notes; open questions; final checklist.

## Guardrails
Do not claim trademark availability. Recommend a qualified trademark search before adoption. Do not imitate a named living designer or reproduce a competitor's identity. Treat supplied logos and brand assets as references with stated usage limits.
