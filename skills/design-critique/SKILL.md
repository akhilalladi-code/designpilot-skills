# Design Critique Skill

## Objective
Improve the work without replacing the creator's intent with personal taste.

## Review framework
Assess:
1. **Goal and audience:** Is the intended message appropriate and clear?
2. **Hierarchy:** What is noticed first, second, and third?
3. **Composition:** Are alignment, balance, spacing, and grouping intentional?
4. **Typography:** Are roles, readability, and consistency effective?
5. **Color and contrast:** Is the palette coherent and accessible?
6. **Imagery:** Are crop, quality, relevance, and licensing appropriate?
7. **Brand consistency:** Does the work follow the supplied identity?
8. **Craft and production:** Are details, dimensions, and exports correct?

## Critique method
- Begin with a neutral one-sentence description of what the design appears to communicate.
- Identify 2–3 strengths with specific visual evidence.
- List issues by impact: critical, important, or polish.
- For each issue, describe what is observed, why it matters, and one practical correction.
- Distinguish objective problems from subjective alternatives.
- Prioritise changes that improve comprehension before decorative refinements.

## Output format
**Overall read:** concise summary.

**Working well:** evidence-based strengths.

**Priority fixes:** issue → impact → recommended action.

**Optional explorations:** clearly labelled alternative directions.

**Final QA:** concise checklist.

## Quality checklist
- The critique is tied to the stated communication goal.
- Every major issue includes observable evidence and a practical correction.
- High-impact comprehension issues are prioritised over personal taste.
- Strengths are acknowledged without vague praise.
- Uncertainty and untested accessibility claims are clearly identified.

## Guardrails
Do not infer a designer's ability, intent, or identity from one artifact. Be respectful and specific. Do not claim accessibility compliance without testing. If image detail is insufficient, state what cannot be assessed.
