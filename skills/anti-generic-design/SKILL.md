# Anti-Generic Design Skill

## Objective
Replace default AI design clichés with decisions grounded in the brief, audience, brand, and communication problem.

## Common warning signs
- Unexplained gradients, glass effects, glows, blobs, and floating cards
- Random decorative geometry or meaningless 3D objects
- Generic stock imagery unrelated to the message
- Excessive font weights, colors, shadows, and rounded containers
- Predictable centered hero + three-card layouts used without reason
- Tiny low-contrast text and excessive copy
- Visual metaphors that do not connect to the subject

These treatments are not inherently wrong; they become weak when selected automatically without purpose.

## Workflow
1. Extract the specific nouns, audience, context, and emotional tone from the brief.
2. Write the core communication idea in one sentence.
3. Generate at least three distinct visual territories using different composition or storytelling approaches.
4. For each visual choice, explain its function: hierarchy, meaning, navigation, emphasis, or atmosphere.
5. Remove any element that has no clear role.
6. Add specificity through real product details, supplied brand assets, relevant context, and deliberate typography.
7. Check whether the concept could be reused unchanged for an unrelated brand. If yes, make it more specific.
8. Validate legibility, accessibility, and production constraints.

## Originality test
Ask:
- What detail makes this direction belong to this brief?
- What is the central idea beyond "make it modern"?
- Which element carries the message?
- What can be removed without losing meaning?
- Is the design distinctive for a reason, or merely unusual?

## Output format
Provide the core idea, three visual territories, rationale for each, recommended route, specific design decisions, elements deliberately excluded, and a QA checklist.

## Quality checklist
- The concept is specific to the brief and audience.
- Each major visual element has a clear communication role.
- The direction avoids unmotivated visual clichés.
- Distinctiveness comes from relevant content and coherent choices.
- The design remains legible, accessible, and production-ready.

## Guardrails
Do not add visual complexity just to appear original. Do not imitate a named artist or designer. Distinctiveness should come from relevant content and coherent choices, not arbitrary novelty.
