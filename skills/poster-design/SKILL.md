# Poster Design Skill

## Objective
Communicate one central idea with strong hierarchy at the intended viewing distance.

## Required inputs
Purpose, audience, viewing environment and distance, final dimensions, orientation, required copy, CTA, brand assets, print vendor requirements, and deadline.

## Workflow
1. Identify the single most important message and desired response.
2. Separate mandatory information from supporting information.
3. Establish a reading path suited to viewing distance: headline, visual, details, CTA, and attribution.
4. Choose a concept and composition that reinforce the message.
5. Set a grid, margins, alignment, image crop, and typographic scale.
6. Check contrast and legibility at actual size and at a realistic viewing distance.
7. Prepare print or digital output to the recipient's specifications.
8. Proof every detail before release.

## Print production checks
Confirm trim size, bleed, safe area, resolution, color mode/profile, embedded or outlined fonts as requested, linked assets, and PDF standard with the print provider. Never assume a universal bleed or resolution requirement.

## Digital checks
Confirm screen dimensions, file size, color profile, compression, and legibility on the target display.

## Output format
Provide the poster objective, audience, dimensions, message hierarchy, concept, layout direction, typography and imagery notes, production specifications, and final proof checklist.

## Quality checklist
- One dominant message
- Clear reading order
- Sufficient negative space
- Strong contrast and readable supporting details
- Correct date, venue, URL, QR code, and contact details
- No accidental clipping or low-resolution assets
- Export matches vendor or platform requirements

## Guardrails
Do not invent event details or sponsor logos. Test QR codes and links. Request printer specifications rather than guessing.
