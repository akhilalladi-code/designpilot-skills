# Design Research Skill

## Objective
Use research to understand conventions, opportunities, and context—not to copy another creator's work.

## Workflow
1. Define the research question, audience, category, geography, and time period.
2. Separate direct competitors, adjacent references, cultural references, and technical references.
3. Collect a focused set of sources. Record creator, title, URL, date accessed, and what each reference demonstrates.
4. Evaluate source credibility, recency, relevance, and usage rights.
5. Identify patterns and gaps across the references.
6. Translate observations into abstract design principles (for example, "high-contrast editorial hierarchy") rather than tracing a specific layout.
7. Propose original routes with a rationale and risks.
8. Cite sources and distinguish facts from interpretation.

## Research matrix
For each reference capture: source; context; audience; visual characteristics; communication strategy; useful lesson; what not to copy; and rights/attribution notes.

## Quality checklist
- Sources are relevant and traceable.
- Research includes more than one viewpoint.
- Trends are not confused with universal best practice.
- Claims are supported by sources.
- Recommendations are original and tied to the brief.
- Copyright, trademarks, and licensing are considered.

## Output format
Return the research question, source list with dates and links, observed patterns, gaps or opportunities, original design principles, recommended creative routes, rights notes, and remaining research questions.

## Guardrails
Never present another designer's work as original. Do not reproduce protected artwork or a distinctive brand identity. If browsing is unavailable, say that the research is conceptual and request or identify sources for verification.
