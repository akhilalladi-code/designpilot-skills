# Practical examples

These examples show how to give DesignPilot Skills enough context to produce useful, reviewable design work. Replace the sample details with your own brief and attach approved brand assets when available.

## 1. Solar product launch announcement

**Use:** `skills/social-media-design/SKILL.md`, then `skills/brand-identity/SKILL.md` for brand consistency.

**Brief**

- Brand: SunPeak Solar (fictional example)
- Product: High-efficiency solar module
- Audience: Solar installers, distributors, and project developers
- Platform: LinkedIn
- Format: 1080 × 1080 px
- Objective: Announce a new product without making unsupported performance claims
- Required content: Product name, launch message, website placeholder, contact placeholder
- Constraints: Use only approved brand colors and product photography; keep all text readable on mobile

**Prompt**

```text
Follow the DesignPilot social media design skill.

Create a LinkedIn launch-announcement concept for the fictional solar brand
SunPeak Solar. Start by identifying the audience, one key message, and the
visual hierarchy. Then propose two distinct layout directions.

For the selected direction, provide:
1. A layout specification with dimensions and safe margins
2. Headline, supporting copy, and CTA placeholders
3. Image treatment and typography guidance
4. A production checklist for mobile legibility and export

Do not invent certifications, efficiency figures, warranties, or customer claims.
Mark missing facts as placeholders for approval.
```

**Review before publishing:** Verify every product specification, claim, URL, and contact detail with an authorized source.

## 2. Starter brand identity direction

**Use:** `skills/brand-identity/SKILL.md` and `skills/color-systems/SKILL.md`.

**Prompt**

```text
Follow the DesignPilot brand identity and color systems skills.

Develop an initial identity direction for a fictional neighborhood coffee
brand called Fieldnote Coffee.

Audience: students and remote workers.
Personality: thoughtful, warm, modern, unpretentious.
Applications: storefront sign, takeaway cup, menu, and Instagram.

Deliver:
- A concise positioning statement and three brand attributes
- Two visual territories with rationale
- A preliminary color palette with HEX values and usage roles
- Typography direction and hierarchy
- Logo-system requirements and practical usage constraints

Do not present exploratory concepts as a finished trademark-cleared identity.
Flag any contrast or legibility issues.
```

## 3. Event poster planning

**Use:** `skills/poster-design/SKILL.md` and `skills/typography/SKILL.md`.

**Prompt**

```text
Follow the DesignPilot poster design and typography skills.

Plan a poster for a community design meetup.
Format: A3 portrait.
Audience: beginner and intermediate designers.
Required information: event title, date, time, venue, registration URL.
Primary objective: make the event name and date understandable at a glance.

Give me:
- A ranked content hierarchy
- Two composition directions
- A grid and spacing plan
- Type sizes and contrast recommendations
- Print production checks, including bleed and safe area

Use placeholders for any event details not supplied. Do not invent speakers
or sponsors.
```

## 4. Structured design critique

**Use:** `skills/design-critique/SKILL.md` and, where relevant, `skills/anti-generic-design/SKILL.md`.

**Prompt**

```text
Follow the DesignPilot design critique skill.

Review the attached design against this brief:
[Paste the approved brief.]

Assess hierarchy, message clarity, typography, color and contrast,
composition, brand consistency, accessibility, and production readiness.

For each issue:
- Describe what is visible rather than guessing intent
- Explain its impact on the audience
- Give one specific improvement
- Assign priority: critical, high, medium, or low

Separate objective problems from subjective preferences. End with the
three changes that would improve the design most.
```

## How to get better results

- Supply the actual audience, objective, dimensions, copy, and constraints.
- Attach only assets you have permission to use.
- Provide official brand guidelines instead of asking the model to guess.
- Ask for a layout specification before requesting a finished visual.
- Verify factual claims, accessibility, licensing, and print/export settings.
