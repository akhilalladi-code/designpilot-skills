# Design case studies

These are **illustrative teaching examples**, not client projects or measured user studies. They demonstrate how DesignPilot Skills can improve the clarity and usefulness of a design workflow. The before-and-after comparisons describe the brief and proposed design direction; they do not claim that a finished visual asset was produced or tested.

## Case study 1 — Solar product launch announcement

**Skills applied:** Social media design, brand identity, typography, color systems, anti-generic design.

### Starting brief

A solar manufacturer wants a LinkedIn announcement for a new solar module. The audience includes distributors, installers, and project developers.

### Before: an under-specified request

> Make a modern and attractive solar panel launch poster. Use green colors and add some solar images.

**Why this is weak**

- "Modern" and "attractive" do not define a visual direction.
- The audience and primary message are not prioritized.
- No dimensions, approved copy, logo rules, or asset constraints are supplied.
- A generic green gradient and stock solar photograph could look interchangeable with many other brands.
- Product claims might be invented if the assistant is not given verified facts.

### After: a production-aware creative brief

**Objective:** Announce the module and make the product name and launch message easy to understand in a LinkedIn feed.

**Audience:** Solar distributors, installers, EPC teams, and project developers.

**Format:** 1080 × 1080 px, with a mobile-first text hierarchy.

**Visual direction:** A confident product-led composition. Use approved module photography as the hero, a restrained brand-color field, and a clear information panel. Avoid decorative solar icons unless they add meaning.

**Hierarchy:**
1. Approved launch headline
2. Product name
3. One verified product benefit, if supplied
4. Supporting launch information
5. Website or contact CTA

**Production requirements:** Use official logo files, approved fonts and colors, verified product copy, sufficient contrast, safe margins, and a final export review.

**Prompt to reuse**

```text
Follow the DesignPilot social media design, brand identity, and
typography skills.

Plan a LinkedIn product launch announcement for a solar module.

Audience: distributors, installers, EPC teams, and project developers.
Format: 1080 × 1080 px.
Use only the brand assets and approved product facts I provide.

First propose the message hierarchy and two distinct layout directions.
Then provide a detailed creative specification, copy placement,
image treatment, typography guidance, and production checklist.

Do not invent efficiency figures, certifications, warranties, or
performance claims. Mark missing information as a placeholder.
```

**What improved:** The request now defines audience, objective, format, content hierarchy, brand constraints, and factual guardrails before visual styling begins.

## Case study 2 — Community event poster

**Skills applied:** Poster design, typography, design critique, anti-generic design.

### Starting brief

A local design community needs an A3 poster to promote a meetup.

### Before: an under-specified request

> Design a creative poster for a design event. Make it eye-catching with bold fonts.

**Why this is weak**

- The event title, date, venue, and registration details have no defined priority.
- "Bold fonts" is a styling instruction, not a complete hierarchy.
- No print requirements are specified.
- Decorative effects could compete with essential event information.

### After: a structured poster specification

**Objective:** Help a viewer understand what the event is and when and where it happens within a few seconds.

**Audience:** Beginner and intermediate designers.

**Format:** A3 portrait. Confirm the printer's bleed and color requirements before final artwork.

**Visual direction:** A strong typographic composition with one distinctive graphic idea connected to the event theme. Keep supporting details calm and highly legible.

**Hierarchy:**
1. Event title
2. Date and time
3. Venue
4. Short event description
5. Registration URL or QR code

**Layout:** Use a consistent grid, clear alignment, generous spacing, and a visible safe area. Test the poster at both full size and reduced viewing size.

**Production requirements:** Confirm final copy, verify the QR code and URL, check image rights, confirm print profile with the vendor, and export with the requested bleed and crop marks.

**Prompt to reuse**

```text
Follow the DesignPilot poster design and typography skills.

Plan an A3 portrait poster for a community design meetup.

Audience: beginner and intermediate designers.
Required information: event title, date, time, venue, registration URL.
The event details will be supplied separately; do not invent them.

Give me a content hierarchy, two layout directions, a grid and spacing
plan, typography recommendations, and a print production checklist.
Explain how the design will remain legible when viewed from a distance.
```

**What improved:** The poster is now planned around communication priorities and print constraints, not just visual effects.

## Reusable before-and-after review method

Use this method when documenting future examples:

1. **Capture the original brief.** Keep it unchanged so the starting point is clear.
2. **Identify missing information.** Note audience, objective, message, format, assets, constraints, and approval requirements.
3. **Apply relevant skills.** Select only the workflows that directly address the gaps.
4. **Write the improved specification.** Explain the design decisions and production requirements.
5. **Review the result.** Separate observable improvements from subjective preferences.
6. **Label evidence honestly.** Distinguish a proposed direction, a generated draft, a completed design, and a user-tested result.

## Contributing a case study

A useful contribution should include the original brief, the relevant skill files, the revised specification, and a concise explanation of what changed. Obtain permission before sharing client work, remove confidential information, and label fictional or AI-generated examples accurately.
